Dairy Processing Systems & Pasteurization Skids

APS procures, fabricates, and supplies sanitary dairy processing machinery and complete line solutions: automated HTST plate pasteurizers, high-pressure homogenizers, centrifugal clarifiers, and aseptic tank farms for milk, yogurt, laban, and cheese production across the UAE, Saudi Arabia, and the GCC.

What are the critical automation and hygienic requirements for industrial dairy pasteurization and processing systems?

Industrial dairy pasteurization and processing systems require automated High-Temperature Short-Time (HTST) plate pasteurizers operating at 72°C–75°C with a 15–20 second validated holding tube and fail-safe flow diversion valves (FDV) monitored by redundant RTD sensors. Systems integrate sanitary two-stage homogenizers operating up to 250 bar, centrifugal clarifiers with automated partial de-sludging, and aseptic stainless steel tank farms with automated Clean-in-Place (CIP) and Sterilize-in-Place (SIP) sequences governed by ISA-88 recipe management.

Hygienic Processing Machinery Modules

01

Automated HTST Pasteurizer Skids

Continuous plate pasteurization skids with up to 92% regenerative heat recovery. Equipped with sanitary booster pumps to maintain positive differential pressure on the pasteurized side, calibrated holding tubes (15s–30s), dual RTD sensors, and pneumatic Flow Diversion Valves (FDV) ensuring zero under-pasteurized discharge.

High-Pressure Homogenizers

Two-stage homogenizers operating up to 250 bar (3,600 PSI) with wear-resistant tungsten carbide or stellite valve assemblies. Effectively reduces fat globule size below 1 micron for smooth milk mouthfeel, improved viscosity in laban and yogurt, and prevention of fat separation during extended storage.

Centrifugal Clarifiers & Separators

High-efficiency disc stack centrifuges running at 5,000–7,000 RPM for cold or warm milk clarifying and skimming. Features automatic partial and full de-sludging, hermetic feed systems to eliminate air incorporation, and precision density transmitters for automated cream fat standardization.

04

Aseptic Tanks & Mixproof Valve Clusters

Sanitary 316L stainless steel storage silos, fermentation tanks, and aseptic buffer tanks with laser-welded dimple cooling jackets and sterile air filtration. Interconnected via double-seat mixproof valve matrixes allowing concurrent product transfer and CIP cleaning without risk of cross-contamination.

03 Process FAQs

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to critical process engineering questions regarding pasteurization compliance, homogenizer operation, and CIP validation.

What temperature and holding regimes are standard for APS HTST pasteurizers?

+

APS pasteurization skids are configured for High-Temperature Short-Time (HTST) pasteurization at 72°C–75°C with a 15–20 second holding tube, Extended Shelf Life (ESL) at 125°C–130°C for 2–4 seconds, and yogurt milk preparation at 85°C–90°C with 5-minute holding for whey protein denaturation.

How is cross-contamination prevented between raw and pasteurized milk?

+

Cross-contamination is prevented by maintaining a positive differential pressure on the pasteurized milk side using a sanitary centrifugal booster pump. If pressure drops below raw milk pressure, or if temperature falls below the pasteurization setpoint, a fail-safe pneumatic Flow Diversion Valve (FDV) instantly redirects product back to the balance tank.

What energy recovery efficiency is achievable in dairy plate heat exchangers?

+

APS multi-section plate heat exchangers achieve up to 92% thermal regeneration efficiency by utilizing incoming cold raw milk to pre-cool outgoing hot pasteurized milk, dramatically reducing plant steam and chilled water utility consumption.

Which automation standards govern dairy process control and recipe management?

+

Dairy line automation is built in compliance with ANSI/ISA-88 (S88) batch control standards on Siemens S7-1500 or Rockwell ControlLogix platforms, featuring automated CIP routing, recipe-driven ingredient addition, and electronic batch recording compliant with FDA 21 CFR Part 11.
